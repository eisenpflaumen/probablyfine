# Education Options outside Luxembourg

Many people combine the financial advantages of working in Luxembourg with the financial and other advantages of living elsewhere, or splitting residence.  There are multiple different strategies to get the best of both worlds, and this set of options can seem dauntingly complicated.

No one person has experience of all possible lifestyles, below is an attempt to collect salient facts which can help individual families to make their own decisions.

## Access to benefits (caisse de l'avenir)

Parents working in Luxembourg receive (approximately) 350 euro per month per child. The general principle seems to be that this income is tied to employment of the parent, not residence, however if one or both parents are eligible for comparable benefits from a different country then this can disqualify them.

## Residence and Tax

If you work in Luxembourg but live elsewhere for a substantial part of the year (for the UK it is ninety days, in other jurisdictions, rules may be different) then a calculation begins in which income tax and pension insurance need to be paid in the other jurisdiction. In theory this is proportional to the time spent, however it can get complicated. Two parents earning in different countries is a particular challenge although simplifications of the Lux tax code to facilitate this arrangement are expected for 2028.

## Can children cross the border to go to school outside Luxembourg?

There is no legal obstacle to this and it is popular for children to cross the border in both directions to attend school. Places in state schools are not usually offered to non-resident children as a priority, but they are offered in many cases.

 ## Can children and (one or both) parents have separate primary residence?

There is no legal obstacle to this, however there may be practical difficulties and emotional difficulties would seem to be inherent. Wealthy families have traditionally solved this problem by sending their children to boarding school, the alternative is to split the family and have relatives, or only one of two parents, care for the children while they attend school. Luxembourg's economy is geared to attracting the skilled and the wealthy through tax incentives, high wages, and soft inducements such as the large density of Michelin starred restaurants. 
Unfortunately even the proudest defenders of Luxembourg's education system cannot argue that it is focused on providing élite schooling to match the aspiration to have an an élite workforce.
